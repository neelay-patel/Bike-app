# The research behind what the coach says

Train's "why it works" lines and its praise (`rideengine/analysis/voice.py`, docs/train-method.md step 5) each
have a key; this file holds the evidence for each, so a rider can ask "says who?" and a qualified coach can check
us. Started 23 Nov 2026. **Status: a first pass from the published literature, not yet reviewed by a coach**; the
review is a must before other riders use Train (docs/train-tab.md, "Method: the credential"). Strength of evidence:
**strong** (several controlled studies or a consensus statement), **moderate** (some studies, consistent with
physiology), **practice** (how good coaches work; little direct study).

The words stay plain on purpose (docs/train-words.md): the lines simplify, and this file says how far.

## Punch (short hard efforts, short rests)
### `punch-recover`
"Short hard efforts with short rests teach you to recover while still riding: exactly what a group's surges ask."
Repeated short efforts with incomplete recovery train the ability to reproduce high power after a hard effort
(repeated-sprint and high-intensity interval research). **Moderate.**
- Bishop D, Girard O, Mendez-Villanueva A (2011). Repeated-sprint ability, part II: recommendations for training.
  *Sports Medicine* 41(9): 741–756.
- Buchheit M, Laursen PB (2013). High-intensity interval training, solutions to the programming puzzle, part I.
  *Sports Medicine* 43(5): 313–338.

### `punch-repeat`
"Repeating hard minutes raises how much you can give above your limit, and how fast you bounce back."
Short intervals above threshold improve high-intensity capacity and power at the top of the range in trained
cyclists. **Moderate.**
- Rønnestad BR, Hansen J, Vegge G, Tønnessen E, Slettaløkken G (2015). Short intervals induce superior training
  adaptations compared with long intervals in cyclists: an effort-matched approach. *Scandinavian Journal of
  Medicine & Science in Sports* 25(2): 143–151.
- Laursen PB, Jenkins DG (2002). The scientific basis for high-intensity interval training. *Sports Medicine*
  32(1): 53–73.

### `punch-rest-short`
"The easy minutes between are short on purpose: recovering under pressure is a skill." The work-to-rest ratio
shapes what an interval session trains; short rests keep the effort's demand high across the set. **Moderate**
(the physiology is clear; "a skill" is our plain-words framing).
- Buchheit & Laursen (2013), above.

## Top end (3 to 5 minute efforts)
### `vo2-ceiling`
"Efforts of 3 to 5 minutes near your limit raise the most oxygen your body can use." Intervals of about 3–5 minutes
at high intensity raise maximal oxygen uptake more than moderate continuous training. **Strong.**
- Helgerud J et al. (2007). Aerobic high-intensity intervals improve VO2max more than moderate training. *Medicine
  & Science in Sports & Exercise* 39(4): 665–671.
- Buchheit & Laursen (2013), above.

### `vo2-breathing`
"Breathing hard for minutes at a time is the work itself." Time spent near maximal oxygen uptake is held to be the
main stimulus of these sessions. **Moderate** (widely used; the exact dose is debated).
- Buchheit & Laursen (2013), above.

## Hold (steady work just below the limit)
### `hold-below`
"Long steady blocks just below your limit raise what you can hold for an hour, without the fatigue of going over."
"Sweet spot" is a practitioners' idea: work just below threshold gives much of threshold training's benefit for
less fatigue. **Practice**; note the counter-evidence that elite endurance athletes do little training here, most
of it easy and some hard (see `easy-most`), and that polarized training did better than threshold-heavy training in
one controlled study.
- Allen H, Coggan A. *Training and Racing with a Power Meter* (book; the sweet spot idea).
- Stöggl T, Sperlich B (2014). Polarized training has greater impact on key endurance variables than threshold,
  high intensity, or high volume training. *Frontiers in Physiology* 5: 33.

### `hold-even`
"The same watts from the first minute to the last." Even pacing is close to optimal for steady efforts of this
length. **Moderate.**
- Abbiss CR, Laursen PB (2008). Describing and understanding pacing strategies during athletic competition.
  *Sports Medicine* 38(3): 239–252.

### `hold-duration`
"Ten minutes or more at a time." Threshold and sweet spot work is usually prescribed in blocks of 8–20 minutes or
more. **Practice.**

## Long (hours at an easy pace)
### `long-engine`
"Hours at an easy pace grow the small blood vessels and the energy factories in your legs." Endurance training
increases muscle capillaries and mitochondria. **Strong.**
- Holloszy JO, Coyle EF (1984). Adaptations of skeletal muscle to endurance exercise and their metabolic
  consequences. *Journal of Applied Physiology* 56(4): 831–838.

### `long-fuel`
"Long easy rides teach your body to burn more fat." Endurance training raises fat use at a given effort.
**Strong.**
- Holloszy & Coyle (1984), above.
- Achten J, Jeukendrup AE (2004). Optimizing fat oxidation through exercise and diet. *Nutrition* 20(7–8):
  716–727.

### `long-eat`
"You're training your stomach as well as your legs." The gut adapts to taking in carbohydrate during exercise.
**Moderate.**
- Jeukendrup AE (2017). Training the gut for athletes. *Sports Medicine* 47(Suppl 1): 101–110.

## Easy, rest and recovery
### `easy-most`
"Most riding should be easy: it lets the hard days be truly hard." Successful endurance athletes do most of their
training at low intensity, with a smaller share hard. **Strong** (descriptive studies, and controlled studies of
polarized training).
- Seiler KS, Kjerland GØ (2006). Quantifying training intensity distribution in elite endurance athletes: is there
  evidence for an "optimal" distribution? *Scandinavian Journal of Medicine & Science in Sports* 16(1): 49–56.
- Seiler S (2010). What is best practice for training intensity and duration distribution in endurance athletes?
  *International Journal of Sports Physiology and Performance* 5(3): 276–291.
- Stöggl & Sperlich (2014), above.

### `zone2`
"Zone 2: the hardest you can ride while still below your first threshold." Experts agree on the idea (just under
the first lactate or ventilatory threshold) more than on how to find it; fixed percentages of max heart rate miss it
for many riders, which is why Kova starts from heart rate reserve, lets the rider set their own, and checks it by
heart-rate drift. **Moderate.** (Authors and full references to fill in at the coach's review.)
- "What Is 'Zone 2 Training'?: Experts' Viewpoint on Definition, Training Methods, and Expected Adaptations" (2025).
  *International Journal of Sports Physiology and Performance*. PubMed 40010355.
- "Zone 2 Intensity: A Critical Comparison of Individual Variability in Different Submaximal Exercise Intensity
  Boundaries" (2025), 50 cyclists. PubMed 40225831.

### `easy-chat`
"If you could chat all the way, you're doing it right." The talk test is a reasonable field marker of low
intensity. **Moderate.**
- Seiler (2010), above (intensity zones and their markers).

### `recover-builds`
"Fitness is built while you recover." Adaptation follows training plus recovery; too little recovery leads to
overreaching and worse. **Strong** (consensus statement).
- Meeusen R et al. (2013). Prevention, diagnosis, and treatment of the overtraining syndrome: joint consensus
  statement of the European College of Sport Science and the American College of Sports Medicine. *Medicine &
  Science in Sports & Exercise* 45(1): 186–205.

### `rest-sleep`
"A rest day and a good night's sleep do more for tomorrow than an extra hour." Sleep matters to performance and
recovery. **Moderate** ("more than an extra hour" is our framing, not a measured comparison).
- Fullagar HHK et al. (2015). Sleep and athletic performance. *Sports Medicine* 45(2): 161–186.

## What the coach praises (and why it's worth praising)
### `paced-set`
The last hard effort as strong as the first: the set was paced, not front-loaded. Even pacing across repeated
efforts keeps quality through the set. **Moderate.** Abbiss & Laursen (2008); Bishop et al. (2011), above.

### `more`
More of the job than the week asked: extra work done. Praised as effort, not as a target to beat every time; the
limits (rest between hard days) still apply. **Practice.**

### `even-blocks`
Steady blocks within a few watts of each other. Abbiss & Laursen (2008), above. **Moderate.**

### `quick-legs`
90 rpm or more through hard efforts. Trained cyclists choose high cadences at high power; a quicker cadence lowers
the force per pedal stroke. **Moderate** (the best cadence is individual).
- Lucía A, Hoyos J, Chicharro JL (2001). Preferred pedalling cadence in professional cycling. *Medicine & Science
  in Sports & Exercise* 33(8): 1361–1366.

### `low-drift`
Heart rate rising little late in a long ride at the same power ("cardiovascular drift"). Less drift at a given
effort goes with better endurance and enough fluid. **Moderate.**
- Coyle EF, González-Alonso J (2001). Cardiovascular drift during prolonged exercise: new perspectives. *Exercise
  and Sport Sciences Reviews* 29(2): 88–92.

### `kept-easy`
An easy day ridden easy. Seiler (2010); Stöggl & Sperlich (2014), above. **Strong.**
